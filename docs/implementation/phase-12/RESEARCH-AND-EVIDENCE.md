# Research and evidence

## Questions before solutions

Find the recurring job parents struggle to finish after finding a recipe. Ask whether the problem concerns choosing, cooking, remembering, planning or understanding the recipe. Do not start by asking whether users want AI or a planner. Separate requests for more content from problems navigating existing content.

The research output is a problem brief with evidence, affected segment, alternatives, frequency, consequence and limitations. A feature list without evidence does not pass the selection gate.

## Phase 11 evidence intake

Collect the named release, observation dates, genuine order counts, mature return cohorts, refund/support reasons, failed searches, recipe feedback and data freshness. Include the exact paid offer version and accessible recipe count during the observation period. A library with three available recipes has different limits from a purchased collection.

Read [Phase 11 first-month definitions](../phase-11/FIRST-30-DAYS.md) and [Phase 9 measurement](../phase-9/MEASUREMENT-AND-EVENTS.md) before calculating new metrics. Keep captured payments and access operations separate from optional behavioural analytics. Never fill a missing metric with zero.

| Evidence | Useful inference | Inference to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated support questions about finding dinners | Navigation or catalog gap worth investigating | Automatic demand for a planner |
| Recipe opens and print requests | Interest and intended use | Confirmed cooking or eating |
| Parents describing repeated manual weekly lists | A recurring planning workflow | Willingness to pay without a price test |
| Instagram saves/comments | Topic resonance among the observed audience | Web conversion or buyer retention |
| Few purchases | Limited commercial evidence | Proof of no demand without checking reach, offer and failures |
| Native feature exists | Potential implementation reuse | Demand, correctness or production readiness |

## Recruitment proposal

Proposed first round: 8–12 adult parents across buyers who returned, buyers who did not return, and free visitors who did not purchase. Include several phone users arriving through Instagram. Report actual counts and recruitment source, including gaps. These counts support exploratory interviews, not population estimates.

Use authorised channels and opt-in contact only. Draft recruitment text in the repo, but do not send messages from this plan task. A recipe purchase, Instagram follow or support email does not automatically authorise unrelated research outreach. Obtain separate permission for recording, quotes and later marketing use.

Avoid recruiting children. Do not request child names, photos, birth dates, diagnoses or feeding records. Redirect volunteered sensitive details toward the parent's workflow and omit those details from research notes. If a user requests individual health advice, route through the established support/content process rather than turning research into care.

## Interview script

1. “Think about the last time you chose a toddler meal. What happened from deciding to serving?”
2. “Where did you look first? What made the choice easier or harder?”
3. “Show or describe how you kept track of the recipe. Please leave private family details out.”
4. “How often did this problem happen in the last two weeks?”
5. “What did you do instead? How much effort did the workaround take?”
6. “What happened after opening a My Curated Haven recipe?”
7. “What was missing or unclear?”
8. Only after the problem discussion, show one neutral prototype. “What would you do next? What do you expect this action to do?”
9. “What would stop you from returning to this workflow?”

Do not lead with a preferred feature, promise availability or equate politeness with purchase intent. Record hesitation, misunderstanding and rejection alongside positive reactions.

## Research data contract

Use restricted notes with pseudonymous participant IDs. Store contact information separately from observations. Repo summaries contain aggregate counts and paraphrased themes without personal details. Record purpose, access owner, consent scope, deletion date and whether recordings exist. Proposed retention: remove raw recordings after synthesis and review, within 30 days. Confirm the policy before collection. No recording is required for participation.

Each observation includes participant segment, source, date, problem, frequency, workaround, task outcome and confidence. Do not create invented quotes or testimonials. Differentiate a direct observation from interpretation and product preference.

## Evidence sufficiency

Before selecting a pilot, require recurring examples from more than one household and more than one evidence source, a usable prototype result, and an explanation of why an existing recipe fix is insufficient. Suggested discovery floor: five independent households describe the same recurring problem, supported by observed behaviour or support records. This is a prioritisation heuristic, not statistical proof. Approve or revise the floor before reviewing results.

If acquisition or conversion is broken, fix the funnel before interpreting weak repeat use. If evidence is too sparse, extend research within the approved effort budget or close with hold. Do not launch a waitlist styled as an available product, charge for unapproved scope or create false scarcity to generate a signal.
