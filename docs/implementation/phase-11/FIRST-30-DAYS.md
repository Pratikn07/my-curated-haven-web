# First 30 days

## Operating cadence

Time begins at the first genuine invited launch, not the plan merge. A stage hold pauses expansion but does not pause support or reconciliation for existing purchases.

| Window | Required routine | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 2 staffed hours of each new stage | Review health/alerts, new attempts, paid access and support every 15 minutes | Stage log and named operator |
| Rest of first 72 hours | Automated critical alerts continuously, proposed hourly staffed review, explicit handoffs | Incident/case log and daily reconciliation |
| Days 4–7 | Daily payment/access, mail, job, backup and support review | Daily operating summary |
| Week 2 | First mature 7-day usage cohort, content confusion, costs and small-sample limitations | Weekly product decision record |
| Weeks 3–4 | Repeat cohort/cost/support review, close recurring defects and improve one bottleneck | Ranked backlog and change evidence |
| Day 30 or later if insufficient evidence | Assess sustainability, unresolved risk and Phase 12 options | Phase 11 closure or extended-operation decision |

These cadences are proposed staffing commitments. Assign real people and coverage before launch. Quiet dashboards do not replace job-freshness and reconciliation checks.

## Metric definitions

| Question | Definition | Interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are payments becoming usable access? | Eligible confirmed orders with effective access, plus latency from provider confirmation | All-order operational measure, exclude only documented policy states |
| Are free recipes useful? | Consented genuine free-detail opens, print requests and voluntary feedback | A view is not cooking. A print request is not completed printing |
| Does the offer lead to payment? | Eligible checkout attempts reaching canonical successful payment within a stated window | Keep pending/cancelled/declined separate. Invited noneligible visitors are not checkout failures |
| Is the product converting visitors? | Consented eligible collection visitors linked to purchase under the Phase 9 contract | Does not estimate all visitors when consent/identity coverage is incomplete |
| Do buyers return? | Mature 7-day cohort with a later-day owned recipe/library event | Browser reset, consent and cross-device gaps cause undercounting |
| Are refunds rising? | Unique orders with successful refunds divided by paid orders in a stated cohort/window, plus amounts by currency | Pending refunds/disputes separate, young cohorts are immature |
| Is support sustainable? | Cases per 100 paid orders, oldest access/payment case and actual resolution times | Show raw counts at low volume, do not hide repeat cases |
| Which Tiny Soho placement helps? | Registered campaign's consented visits/attempts/purchases | Instagram reach is a separate denominator, shared/untagged links stay unknown |

Maintain authoritative captured/refunded totals from provider-verified records regardless of optional consent. Show attributed order subset alongside coverage, never in place of all-order totals. Label missing Instagram snapshots unavailable/stale instead of zero.

## Financial and cost review

For each currency, separate captured payments, successful refunds, dispute adjustments, processor fees and provider payouts. Captured less refunds is not profit, and payout balance is not gross sales. Business accounting/tax treatment follows the owner's approved finance process.

Track hosting, database/connection/storage/egress, mail, monitoring and optional analytics costs against the approved monthly ceiling. Record marginal usage and unusual spikes. Include support/editorial hours so a low infrastructure bill does not hide operational effort.

Do not assume current provider tiers, quotas or costs. Read account limits and pricing at implementation time before paid upgrades. Spend alerts should pause optional exports/promotion or trigger review, not abruptly revoke purchased access.

## Feedback collection

Use an optional short product question after a meaningful interaction, or a consented support/research invitation through an authorised channel. Ask about recipe clarity, time constraints, filtering and purchase/access confusion. Do not collect child medical details for routine product feedback.

Record number of responses, recruitment source, buyer/nonbuyer mix and recurring themes. A few enthusiastic replies are useful qualitative signals, not proof of broad demand. Avoid testimonials or attribution without permission.

## Weekly decision template

1. Question and proposed customer outcome.
2. Cohort/window, denominators, counts and data freshness.
3. Observed issue/opportunity, with support/editorial context.
4. Confidence limits: small sample, consent coverage, missing source or immature cohort.
5. One proposed change, expected effect and guardrail.
6. Owner, rollout method, review date and stop criterion.
7. Observed result and next decision after enough evidence.

Prioritise reliable access, clear recipes and truthful purchase copy before growth experiments. Avoid simultaneous price, membership and landing-page changes because attribution becomes unclear. Any new commercial promise requires a new approved offer version and respects existing purchases.

## Phase 12 entry

Consider parenting expansion only after the recipe experience operates reliably and evidence identifies a real adjacent need. Separate existing-app capabilities from customer demand and from the first recipe purchase entitlement.

Prepare options such as improved recipe discovery, meal-planning workflow or another reviewed collection before broader parenting tools, only when evidence supports them. Record expected benefit, implementation cost, privacy implications and a small validation step. No automatic AI/milestone subscription follows from Phase 11.

If demand evidence is sparse, continue the recipe launch and research. If payment/support remains fragile, stabilise first. Closure records should state the limitation rather than manufacture a success story.

The [detailed Phase 12 package](../phase-12/README.md) defines research, native reuse, selection gates and a conditional pilot. Start with its baseline review. The planner specification is an option, not an approved feature or evidence of demand.
